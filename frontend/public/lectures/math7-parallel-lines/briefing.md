**LESSON 9: PARALLEL LINES & RECOGNITION TESTS**
Keithley Lake • Grade 7 Math • 01 November 2026

Interactive flight briefing: /lectures/math7-parallel-lines/index.html

**Mission objectives**
Identify corresponding, alternate interior and same-side interior angles. Use an angle test to prove that two lines are parallel. Write a reasoned proof and construct a parallel through a point.

**1. Mercury: The setup**
Parallel lines are two distinct straight lines in the same plane that never intersect, even when extended indefinitely: $a \parallel b$.
A transversal $c$ intersects $a$ at $A$ and $b$ at $B$. The interior region lies between the two lines; the exterior lies outside them.

![Angle map: 1 top-right, 2 top-left, 3 bottom-left, 4 bottom-right at both intersections](/images/geometry/parallel_lines_angle_map.svg)

Our angle labels describe positions, not equal measures. At each intersection, 1 is top-right, 2 top-left, 3 bottom-left and 4 bottom-right. The diagram is schematic; never use appearance alone as proof.

**2. Venus: Decode Z, F and U**
Alternate interior angles: both inside and on opposite sides of the transversal. The two Z pairs are $\angle A_3,\angle B_1$ and $\angle A_4,\angle B_2$.
Corresponding angles: the same relative position at the two intersections. The four F pairs are $\angle A_1,\angle B_1$; $\angle A_2,\angle B_2$; $\angle A_3,\angle B_3$; and $\angle A_4,\angle B_4$.
Same-side interior angles: both inside and on the same side of the transversal. The two U pairs are $\angle A_3,\angle B_2$ and $\angle A_4,\angle B_1$.
These pair names apply even when the lines are not parallel. Position alone does not imply equality.

**3. Earth: Recognition tests**
When a transversal crosses two distinct lines in the same plane, any ONE of these conditions proves parallelism:
• Equal alternate interior angles: $\angle A_3=\angle B_1\implies a\parallel b$.
• Equal corresponding angles: $\angle A_1=\angle B_1\implies a\parallel b$.
• Supplementary same-side interior angles: $\angle A_4+\angle B_1=180^\circ\implies a\parallel b$.
• Both lines perpendicular to the same line: $a\perp c,\ b\perp c,\ a\ne b\implies a\parallel b$.

Why the U test works: $\angle A_3+\angle A_4=180^\circ$ because they form a linear pair. If $\angle A_4+\angle B_1=180^\circ$, then $\angle A_3=\angle B_1$. Apply the Z test.
Why the perpendicular test works: the corresponding angles are both $90^\circ$.

**4. Mars: Align the lasers**
The lower corresponding angle is $65^\circ$. Adjust the upper angle to exactly $65^\circ$. Equal corresponding angles prove that the flight paths are parallel.
If the corresponding angles differ, the lines are not parallel. Remember that a line extends beyond the displayed segment.

**5. Jupiter: Three-line proof**
Given: $c$ intersects $a$ and $b$, and $\angle A_1,\angle B_1$ are corresponding, both $60^\circ$.
1. Value check: $\angle A_1=\angle B_1=60^\circ$.
2. Position check: these angles are corresponding.
3. Conclusion: $a\parallel b$ by the equal corresponding angle test.

**6. Saturn: Worked examples**
Example 1: $\angle A_3$ and $\angle B_1$ are alternate interior angles, both $75^\circ$. They are equal, so $a\parallel b$.
Example 2: $\angle A_1=110^\circ$ and $\angle B_2=70^\circ$. Since $\angle B_1,\angle B_2$ form a linear pair, $\angle B_1=180^\circ-70^\circ=110^\circ$. Now $\angle A_1=\angle B_1$. They are corresponding, so $a\parallel b$.
Example 3: corresponding angles measure $(2x+10)^\circ$ and $70^\circ$. For parallel lines, $2x+10=70$, so $x=30$. Check: $2(30)+10=70$.

**7. Uranus: Construct a parallel through P**
1. Align one edge of a set square with the given line. Hold a straightedge against another edge.
2. Keep the straightedge fixed and slide the set square until the drawing edge passes through P. Do not rotate it.
3. Draw the new line through P. The original and new lines make equal corresponding angles with the fixed straightedge, so they are parallel.

**8. Neptune: Check your understanding**
• Which pair has a Z pattern? Alternate interior angles.
• What do two top-right angles at different intersections form? A corresponding pair.
• An alternate interior angle is $55^\circ$. What matching measure proves parallelism? $55^\circ$.
• Corresponding angles are $120^\circ$ and $115^\circ$. Parallel? No: unequal corresponding angles.
• Two distinct coplanar lines are perpendicular to c. Relationship? Parallel.
• $\angle A_1=70^\circ$, $\angle A_3$ is vertically opposite, and $\angle B_1=70^\circ$. Why parallel? $\angle A_3=70^\circ$, giving equal alternate interior angles.
• Same-side interior angles are $100^\circ$ and $80^\circ$. Parallel? Yes: their sum is $180^\circ$.
• Corresponding angles are $(2x+10)^\circ$ and $70^\circ$. Find x for parallelism: $x=30$.

**Mission warnings**
Equal vertical angles at ONE intersection cannot prove two different lines parallel. Two obtuse angles need not be equal. Same-side interior angles must sum to $180^\circ$, not necessarily equal each other. Always name the position and justify the numerical relationship before concluding.
